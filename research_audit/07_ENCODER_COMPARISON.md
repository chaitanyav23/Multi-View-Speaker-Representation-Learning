## 1. Detailed Architectural Comparison: TitaNet-Large vs. WeSpeaker ResNet293-LM (`Phase E1 & E2`)

Both primary candidate encoders were audited at the source-code level (`nemo.collections.asr.models.label_models.EncDecSpeakerLabelModel` / `titanet-large.yaml` and `wespeaker/models/resnet.py` / `wespeaker/cli/speaker.py` / `wespeaker/cli/hub.py`):

| Attribute | Encoder 1: **NVIDIA TitaNet-Large** (`titanet_large`) | Encoder 2: **WeSpeaker ResNet293-LM** (`english` / `voxceleb_resnet293_LM`) | Architectural & Robustness Implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Framework & Source** | NVIDIA NeMo (`nemo_toolkit` `2.7.3`) | WeSpeaker (`wenet-e2e/wespeaker` PyTorch / ONNX) | Both run natively in PyTorch on CUDA (`float32` / `float16`). |
| **Model Checkpoint Size & Params** | `101.66 MB` (`~25.3M` parameters) | `114.7 MB` (`~28.6M` parameters) | Nearly identical parameter scale (~25–29M params). |
| **Expected Audio Input** | `16,000 Hz`, mono `float32` waveform (`[-1, 1]`) | `16,000 Hz`, mono waveform (multiplied by `32768.0` to 16-bit PCM scale inside `kaldi.fbank`) | **CRITICAL:** WeSpeaker's `extract_embedding_from_pcm(pcm, sample_rate)` multiplies normalized float `[-1, 1]` waveforms by `32768` (`pcm = pcm.to(torch.float) * 32768` if normalized, or expects raw 16-bit scale in `kaldi.fbank`)! |
| **Acoustic Frontend** | `AudioToMelSpectrogramPreprocessor`:<br>• `25 ms` **Hann** window, `10 ms` shift, `n_fft=512`<br>• `80` log-Mel bins<br>• `dither = 1e-5`<br>• **Per-feature mean AND variance normalization (`normalize="per_feature"`)** over each subsegment! | `torchaudio.compliance.kaldi.fbank`:<br>• `25 ms` **Hamming** window, `10 ms` shift<br>• `80` Kaldi Mel bins (`dither = 0.0` at inference)<br>• **Per-feature Cepstral Mean Normalization ONLY (`cmn=True`, no variance normalization!)** | **High Frontend Complementarity (`VERIFIED`):** On short `0.5s` BWC windows containing sudden noise bursts, TitaNet's per-feature variance normalization divides by the burst's standard deviation (compressing speech energy), whereas ResNet293-LM performs mean-only CMN, preserving relative spectral energy dynamics! |
| **Backbone Topology** | **1D Depthwise-Separable Conv (`ConvASREncoder`):**<br>• 1D temporal convolutions across all 80 frequency bins simultaneously<br>• 3 Mega-blocks (`repeat=3`, `filters=1024`, wide temporal kernels `k=7, 11, 15`)<br>• **Global Squeeze-and-Excitation (`se=True`, `se_context_size=-1`)** in every block. | **2D Residual Network (`ResNet293`, `Bottleneck` `[10, 20, 64, 3]`):**<br>• 2D time-frequency convolutions (`3x3` and `1x1` kernels, `m_channels=32` -> `64` -> `128` -> `256` Bottleneck planes, expansion `4`)<br>• Frequency downsampling by $8\times$ (`80 -> 10` freq bins) and temporal downsampling by $8\times$<br>• **No global SE modules** (pure local 2D time-frequency receptive fields). | **Genuine Structural Diversity (`VERIFIED`):**<br>1. TitaNet's 1D convolutions mix all 80 mel bins at Layer 1 and rely on global SE channel reweighting (vulnerable when narrowband BWC siren/wind corrupts global SE statistics).<br>2. ResNet293's 2D convolutions isolate local time-frequency patches across 96 bottleneck blocks before pooling, providing localized time-frequency invariance to narrowband/band-limited noise! |
| **Temporal Pooling Layer** | **Attentive Statistics Pooling (`pool_mode="attention"`):**<br>• `3072` channels -> learned frame attention weights (`attention_channels=128`) -> weighted mean $\mu$ + weighted std $\sigma$ -> `6144`-D. | **Temporal Statistics Pooling (`TSTP`):**<br>• Flattens frequency $\times$ channel (`10 x 1024 = 10240` or `2560`) -> uniform temporal mean $\mu$ + standard deviation $\sigma$ -> `5120`-D. | TitaNet learns soft frame attention inside the pooling layer; ResNet293 pools uniformly across subsegment frames after 8x 2D strided conv downsampling. |
| **Output Embedding Dimension** | **`192-D`** (`BatchNorm1d(6144) -> Linear(6144, 192) -> BatchNorm1d(192)`) | **`256-D`** (`Linear(5120, 256)`, two-layer projection option) | **Different dimensions (`192` vs `256`)** -> requires either cosine-affinity fusion, projection to a shared dimension $d$, or residual alignment into `192-D`. |
| **Pretraining Corpus** | **16,681 speakers** (~3,373 hours):<br>VoxCeleb1, VoxCeleb2, NIST SRE 2004–2008, Switchboard, Fisher, LibriSpeech (`8 kHz` telephonic upsampled + `16 kHz` wideband + RIR/MUSAN augmentation). | **5,994 speakers** (~2,360 hours):<br>VoxCeleb2 `dev` (`16 kHz` celebrity interviews/red-carpet/outdoor YouTube audio + RIR/MUSAN augmentation). | **Crucial Training-Domain Difference (`VERIFIED`):**<br>• TitaNet was trained on **telephonic + clean read speech + VoxCeleb** (`16,681` spks).<br>• ResNet293-LM was trained purely on **in-the-wild YouTube/VoxCeleb2 (`5,994` spks)** with aggressive Large-Margin fine-tuning. |
| **Training Objective** | `AngularSoftmaxLoss` (AAM-Softmax, `scale=30, margin=0.2`, fixed duration chunks `3.0s`). | Stage 1: AAM-Softmax (`scale=32, margin=0.2` on `2.0s` chunks).<br>Stage 2 (**LM Fine-Tuning**): AAM-Softmax (`margin=0.5` on `6.0s` chunks). | ResNet293-LM's Large-Margin (`m=0.5`) stage enforces tighter angular clustering on longer windows (`>=1.0s`), while TitaNet was trained without `6.0s` large-margin adaptation. |
| **VoxCeleb1-O EER (Published)** | `0.68%` EER | **`0.447%` EER** | ResNet293-LM achieves ~34% lower EER on VoxCeleb1-O than TitaNet-Large. |
| **License** | Apache-2.0 (NeMo code) / CC-BY-4.0 (`titanet-l.nemo`) | Apache-2.0 (`wespeaker` code) / CC-BY-4.0 (pretrained weights) | Fully compatible for academic research and publication. |

## 2. Broader Candidate Encoder Comparison (`Rule 5` — Avoiding Premature Architecture Lock-In)

Per **Audit Rule 5**, we must not assume `TitaNet-Large + WeSpeaker ResNet293-LM` is the only or automatically optimal encoder pair. Below we compare four distinct pretrained encoder families available in the Python speech ecosystem:

| Encoder Candidate | Backbone Family | Pretraining Paradigm & Data | Emb Dim | Strengths on BWC Audio | Weaknesses on BWC Audio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. NVIDIA TitaNet-Large** (`titanet_large`) | 1D Depthwise-Separable Conv + SE + Attentive Stats Pooling | Supervised AAM-Softmax on 16,681 spks (VoxCeleb1/2 + Fisher + Switchboard + SRE + LibriSpeech) | `192-D` | Native encoder for NeMo `diar_msdd_telephonic`; includes telephonic/narrowband speech in training. | 1D conv mixes all frequency bands immediately; global SE context degrades under non-stationary BWC noise; negative silhouette (`-0.049`) on `0.5s` `karen` subsegments. |
| **2. WeSpeaker ResNet293-LM** (`voxceleb_resnet293_LM`) | Deep 2D Conv ResNet-293 + TSTP | Supervised AAM-Softmax + Large-Margin (`m=0.5`) on VoxCeleb2 (5,994 spks) | `256-D` | 2D time-frequency local convolutions resist band-limited noise; mean-only CMN avoids variance compression; `0.447%` EER on VoxCeleb1-O. | LM fine-tuning used `6.0s` segments—short `0.5s` Scale-4 subsegments may suffer domain shift relative to `1.5s` Scale-0 windows. |
| **3. SpeechBrain / NeMo ECAPA-TDNN** (`ecapa_tdnn` / `spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb`) | 1D Res2Net TDNN + SE + Multi-layer Feature Aggregation + Attentive Stats Pooling | Supervised AAM-Softmax on VoxCeleb1+2 (7,205 spks) | `192-D` | Same `192-D` output dimension as TitaNet; NeMo includes a native `ecapa_tdnn` speaker model config. | **Low architectural diversity vs. TitaNet:** Both TitaNet and ECAPA-TDNN are 1D TDNN/Conv + Global SE + Attentive Statistics Pooling architectures! Pairing TitaNet with ECAPA-TDNN offers much less structural complementarity than pairing 1D-Conv TitaNet with 2D-Conv ResNet293-LM. |
| **4. Microsoft WavLM-Large SV** (`microsoft/wavlm-large` + X-vector / MHFA head, or WeSpeaker WavLM) | Self-Supervised Transformer (`24` layers, `1024-D`, gated relative positional bias) | SSL Masked Speech Denoising/Prediction on 94,000 hrs (LibriLight + GigaSpeech + VoxPopuli) + VoxCeleb2 fine-tuning | `256-D` | Explicitly pretrained with **overlapping speech and noisy mixture denoising** during SSL pretraining; strong robustness to overlap and reverberation. | Very heavy (`~316M` parameters, `>1.2 GB` FP32 weights, $10\times$ slower extraction across 5 multiscale windows than ResNet293-LM). |

### Technical Conclusion on Candidate Encoder Selection (`VERIFIED` + `INFERRED`):
1. **Why `TitaNet-Large (1D-Conv + SE, 192-D)` + `WeSpeaker ResNet293-LM (2D-Conv Bottleneck, 256-D)` Is the Strongest Primary Pair:**
   - They represent **genuinely distinct inductive biases**: 1D cross-frequency depthwise-separable convolutions with global SE and per-feature variance normalization (`TitaNet-Large`) vs. deep 2D localized time-frequency convolutions with mean-only CMN (`ResNet293-LM`).
   - Both have comparable parameter counts (`25.3M` vs `28.6M`) and fast CNN inference on GPU, enabling identical 5-scale multiscale extraction (`[1.5, 1.2, 1.0, 0.8, 0.5]s`) in `< 3 seconds` per recording on the local RTX 4090.
2. **Scale-Sensitivity Caveat for ResNet293-LM (`IMPORTANT`):**
   - Because `ResNet293-LM` was fine-tuned with Large-Margin (`m=0.5`) on `6.0s` chunks, its discriminative power is expected to be highest on the longer scales (`Scale 0: 1.5s`, `Scale 1: 1.2s`, `Scale 2: 1.0s`) and potentially noisier on the shortest scale (`Scale 4: 0.5s`). This directly motivates **scale- and duration-aware reliability weighting** when fusing TitaNet and ResNet293-LM across the 5 MSDD scales!
